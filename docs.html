What this is

CuteBunBrokers is a 1,255 piece collection on Robinhood Chain with a livestock game attached. You buy bunnies, they generate carrots simply by existing, you spend those carrots feeding them, they get measurably fatter, and once fat enough you can burn one for an instant $CARROT payout.

The joke: they were stock brokers, they quit, and now they are being fattened for market. The mechanic follows the joke exactly, which is the only reason it holds together.

Why a feeding loop instead of passive yield

Passive yield gives nobody a reason to open the app. A feeding loop gives every holder a small daily decision, a visible result on their own art, and a moment weeks later where they choose whether to cash out. That is the entire retention argument.

The core loop

MINT  ->  SNAPSHOT  ->  FEED  ->  FATTEN  ->  BURN  ->  PAID
                            ^                     |
                            +---------------------+
                              carrots recycle into the next bunny
  1. Mint your bunnies during the allowlist or public round.
  2. Snapshot runs at a published block after mint closes. Your holding size decides your pack.
  3. Feed up to two servings per bunny per day. Each serving costs 50 $CARROT and adds 1 weight.
  4. Fatten through five weight classes. The art visibly widens as weight rises.
  5. Burn one bunny per wallet per week, once it reaches Chonky or above.
  6. Paid in the same transaction. $CARROT arrives in your wallet, no claim page.

The number that makes "hold 5" the right answer

Five bunnies produce 10 $CARROT per day. Feeding one bunny at the maximum two servings costs exactly 10 $CARROT per day. So five bunnies fund one bunny eating at full speed, indefinitely, with nothing left over. Four bunnies cannot keep up. Six gives you a surplus. This is why the VIP tier sits at five and not at three or ten.

Why one per week

Without a throttle the collection empties in a weekend and the payout pool is gone before most people have finished fattening their first bunny. At one burn per wallet per week with a 30 day minimum fattening time, the 555 burn cap takes years to approach rather than days. The throttle is what makes the game last.

Founder Deed

Burning does not erase your bunny. Each burn mints a Founder Deed, a soulbound ERC-5192 token that renders the burned bunny's original art and token ID as your permanent profile on the leaderboard, along with the weight it reached.

  • The art stays pinned to IPFS forever
  • Your token ID and final weight are stored on chain in the Deed
  • Non transferable. Nobody can buy it and nobody can take it
  • What you actually give up is the tradeable ERC-721 and its daily carrot income

Why 10 million and not 100 million

The mint price sets the ceiling on what the token can be worth without breaking the burn mechanic. At a $2.50 allowlist price, a 100,000,000 supply would force $CARROT below $0.0001 and the whole token to a $9,500 valuation. Ten million is the supply that lets the unit price sit somewhere sane while keeping the burn honest. The arbitrage section below shows the working.

Sinks

Feeding is the primary sink and it is permanent destruction, not recycling. Secondary sinks: renaming a pen, entering a season, cosmetic badges, and pen upgrades. Target is net deflationary within two quarters of the game opening. If emissions outrun sinks the response is to add sinks. Rewards already earned are never cut retroactively.

What the 555 cap is actually for

Notice that even a total burnout fits inside the allocation. The cap is not there to keep the payout pool solvent. It exists for one reason: to stop the collection being burned out of existence. A game with forty bunnies left has no leaderboard, no secondary market and no reason for anyone to stay.

Feeding claws some of it back. At 300 $CARROT fed per bunny taken to Fat, the expected case destroys about 124,500 $CARROT on the way in, so net emission from burning sits near 905,000 rather than 1,029,800.

The arbitrage math

This is the failure mode most projects discover only after it has happened to them. If burning pays out more than the bunny cost to mint, people who never intended to play will mint the collection out, fatten, burn, dump, and leave.

        P  <  $0.001155 per $CARROT

The rule

Open with 500,000 $CARROT paired against no more than $550 of ETH. Above that ratio, minting purely to farm and dump becomes profitable and the payout pool drains.

A second defence is built in: the 30 day minimum fattening time means the arbitrage is never instant. Anyone attempting it carries a month of price risk before they can extract anything, which prices most of them out on its own.

Security

What we commit to before a single contract goes live on mainnet.

  • Independent security review before mainnet, published when complete
  • Reentrancy guards on every state changing external function, above all BurnPortal
  • Weight and daily caps enforced on chain, never client side
  • Rarity Merkle root committed and published before mint opens
  • BurnPortal pausable behind a 3 of 5 multisig in case a tier bug appears mid window
  • $CARROT mint authority renounced to the three permitted contracts, verified on Blockscout
  • Chainlink VRF for any randomised pack contents. Never block.timestamp
  • The full loop runs on public testnet 46630 before mainnet, open to anyone
  • No contract custodies user ETH after the mint completes

Risk and legal

Burning is irreversible. No recovery path, no support ticket. The type to confirm step is mandatory on every burn surface.

Never open the burn window before the game is playable. A burn that pays into a promise is how projects earn a reputation they do not recover from.

$CARROT is not an investment. It is an in game utility and reward point. It carries no claim on revenue, profit or assets. We make no statement about its future value, promise no exchange listing, and publish the full supply schedule above. The team allocation vests publicly on chain.

Personal data. The waitlist stores your email and wallet address, and nothing else. The email receives launch announcements and the wallet is used for the allowlist and the pack snapshot. Every announcement carries an unsubscribe link.

What we have not solved

  1. Is 555 the right burn cap? The 700 supply floor is a judgement call. It cannot be changed after deployment, so it should be put to the community first.
  2. Does the 30 day fattening time hold attention? Thirty days is a long first session. Testnet should tell us whether people stay or drift.
  3. What happens when carrot income halves in year two? Five bunnies will no longer fund one bunny at full speed. Either the serving cost halves with it or the economy tightens on purpose, and we have not decided which.
  4. Does anyone burn a Legendary? If the rare tiers never get burned, the high payout brackets are decoration and the real economy is Common and Rare only.
